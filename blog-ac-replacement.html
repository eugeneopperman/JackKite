Repair or replace? How to make the call on an aging AC.

AUG 20, 2025 REPLACEMENT 5 MIN READ

Higher energy bills, uneven cooling, breakdowns that keep coming back — at some point every homeowner asks the same question: is this system worth fixing again? Here's how to think it through.

The early warning signs

  • The unit is 10–15 years old or more
  • Repairs have become a recurring line item
  • Cooling is uneven or simply inadequate
  • Energy costs keep climbing without explanation
  • The system runs on outdated R-22 refrigerant — phased out and now impractical (and expensive) to maintain

The $5,000 rule

A useful shorthand: multiply the system's age by the repair cost. If the answer is over $5,000, replacement usually makes better financial sense than the repair. A $600 repair on a 12-year-old unit is a $7,200 answer — that money is better invested in equipment with a fresh warranty and modern efficiency.

Maintenance still matters

Whichever side of the rule you're on, seasonal tune-ups — refrigerant checks, coil cleaning, component lubrication — extend a system's life and catch issues before summer finds them. Simple habits help too: change filters monthly and keep the outdoor unit clear of debris.

If you do replace

  • Insist on proper sizing via a Manual J load calculation — not a guess based on the old unit
  • Modern systems are dramatically more efficient; the monthly savings are real
  • Ask about rebates and tax incentives for high-efficiency equipment

The right answer is different for every house. What matters is making the call with real numbers in front of you — before the hottest week of the year makes it for you.
